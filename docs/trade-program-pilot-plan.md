## 1. Executive Summary

iotty sells a premium, Italian-designed tempered-glass smart switch into a market where the trade — electricians, custom integrators, builders, and designers — decides what actually gets installed in walls. Today iotty is direct-to-consumer only, with no trade pricing, no installer directory, and no professional channel. That is the gap this program closes.

The proposal: launch a national, three-tier **iotty Trade Partners** program with a 90-day pilot built around a **Founding Partner** offer. The pilot goal is 50 approved partners, 25 activated with a demo kit, and 10 producing repeat project orders — enough signal to prove per-partner economics before scaling spend.

The strategy runs in four movements, and the rest of this document is organized around them: **Introduce** (the landing page and first-touch outreach), **Entice** (the founding-partner hook and demo kit), **Sell** (the call flow that converts an application into a first project order), and **Manage** (the operating cadence that turns a first order into a reordering account).

## 2. Why the Trade Channel — and Why Now

**The product sells itself in the hand, not on a webpage.** A backlit glass switch at $90–$200 per gang is an impulse-resistant purchase online, but it is an easy upsell in person: an electrician doing a $30k renovation, an integrator specing a whole-home system, or a designer choosing wall finishes can add iotty to a project where $150/switch is a rounding error on the budget.

**The trade multiplies revenue per relationship.** A consumer buys 1–4 switches once. An active electrician or integrator touches dozens of homes a year; a production builder touches hundreds. One converted partner is worth an entire month of D2C ad spend.

**Timing: pre-order window = founding-partner leverage.** The current US line (1-gang $90, 2-gang $130, 3-gang $165, 4-gang $200, dimmer module $27 required per dimmable switch; black/white; UL #E527071 + TUV; 1-year limited US warranty) is listed as pre-order with estimated October 2026 shipping. *(Verify current status with Bryan — this is from the live site.)* Rather than a liability, this is the pitch: "get founding-partner pricing and a directory listing locked in before the line ships and the program opens to everyone."

**The competition validates the play.** Lutron's Preferred Pro program (free to join, Bronze→Platinum tiers on rolling 12-month logged volume, installer-directory listing, dedicated support line, free training) is a major reason electricians default to Caséta/RA3. Leviton runs My Leviton Pro for smart-home installers; Brilliant runs dedicated builder and custom-installer programs. iotty can't out-spend them, but it can out-margin and out-design them for the premium residential niche.

## 3. Benchmark Snapshot

| Program | Structure | What partners get |
|---|---|---|
| Lutron Preferred Pro | Free; Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum on rolling 12-mo logged installs; no minimums | "Find an Installer" listing, dedicated tech line (Silver+), free training tracks |
| Leviton My Leviton Pro | Support + rewards for smart-home installers | Priority support, rewards on purchases |
| Brilliant | Separate builder program and custom-installer program | Model-home/spec pricing, installer support |
| Designer trade programs (norms) | Application w/ credentials (EIN, resale cert, portfolio) | Retail brands 10–20% off; trade-only & lighting brands 30–50% off; Net 30–60 at upper tiers |

Two lessons drive the design below: **(1)** make joining frictionless like Lutron (free, no minimums to enter), and **(2)** price the discount like a premium lighting brand (meaningful 25–40%), not like a retail loyalty program (10%).

## 4. Proposed Program Structure

### Tiers

| | **Trade** (entry) | **Pro** | **Elite** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualification | Approved application (credential check) | $5k in rolling 12-mo purchases | $15k in rolling 12-mo purchases |
| Discount off MSRP | 25% | 35% | 40% |
| Directory listing on iottytradepartners.com | ✓ | ✓ Featured | ✓ Featured + priority lead routing |
| Support | Priority email | Priority phone/line | Dedicated rep |
| Early access to new products | — | ✓ | ✓ + input on roadmap |
| Payment terms | Card at order | Card at order | Net 30 |
| Co-marketing | Logo/asset kit | Co-branded collateral | Co-op/MDF eligibility, case-study features |

**Partner margin math** (the slide that closes deals): a 2-gang switch at $130 MSRP costs a Trade partner $97.50 and a Pro partner $84.50. Installed and billed at MSRP (or above, bundled into labor), the partner clears $32–$45 per gang box *before* install labor — on a product that takes the same wire time as a $25 commodity switch. Pitch it as: "same 15 minutes of labor, 4× the ticket."

### Application & verification

Free to apply, ~5-minute form on iottytradepartners.com: business name, trade type, EIN or state license #, website/portfolio or license lookup, and (for resale) a resale certificate upload. Approve within 1 business day during the pilot — speed of approval is itself a selling point against big-brand bureaucracy.

### Demo kit (the activation lever)

Approved partners can buy **one demo kit at 50% off**: 1× 2-gang + 1× 1-gang + 1× dimmer module (≈$247 retail → ≈$123). The kit exists to get glass into hands — a partner who installs iotty in their own home or shows it from a demo board sells it. Kit purchase is the pilot's "activation" metric.

### Founding Partner offer (pilot-only)

The first 50 approved partners get: **Pro-tier pricing (35%) for their first 12 months regardless of volume**, a **free 1-gang switch** with their demo kit, a **Founding Partner badge** in the directory, and grandfathered recognition when the program scales. Scarcity is real (50 slots, counted publicly on the site) and it gives the outreach a reason to exist *now*.

## 5. Pilot Scope, Goals & Success Criteria

**Duration:** 90 days from landing-page live. **Geography:** national (US). **Segments:** all four, with electricians and integrators as primary (they buy volume) and builders/designers as secondary (they spec volume).

| Funnel stage | 90-day target | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Applications | 150 | Landing page conversion, outreach reply rate |
| Approved partners | 50 (Founding cap) | Approval rate, time-to-approve |
| Activated (demo kit purchased) | 25 | Activation rate ≥50% |
| First project order | 15 | Avg first order value (target ≥$500) |
| Repeat order | 10 | Reorder rate, 60-day repeat window |
| Partner-sourced revenue | $25k+ | Revenue per active partner |

**Go/no-go at day 90:** if activation ≥40% and reorder ≥30% of activated partners, scale the program (open tiers publicly, add budget). If not, the exit interviews with the 50 partners tell us whether the problem is price, product timing, or pitch — cheap learning either way.

## 6. Segment Playbook

**Electricians / electrical contractors** — *the volume engine.* Pain: commodity switch work is race-to-the-bottom pricing. Angle: iotty is a ticket-builder — "offer a glass upgrade on every panel/reno job." They care about: install speed (standard box, no hub, 10-minute swap), UL cert, and margin. Find them: state license databases, Facebook/Reddit trade groups, supply-house counter relationships, IEC/NECA chapters. Watch-out: many are loyal to Lutron/Leviton through distributor relationships — position iotty as an *additional* premium line, not a replacement.

**Custom integrators (AV/smart home)** — *the natural fit.* Pain: clients want switches that match a $200k smart home aesthetic; Decora plastic doesn't. Angle: iotty is the finish detail that photographs well — and Wi-Fi/no-hub means no programming labor. They care about: reliability, Alexa/Google/Siri support, and whether it embarrasses them at the client walkthrough. Find them: CEDIA directory, HTA-certified firms, r/hometheater and CE Pro communities. Watch-out: integrators will ask about Control4/Crestron drivers and local API — have an honest answer ready.

**Builders / remodelers** — *the multi-unit whale.* Pain: differentiating spec homes; "smart home package" as a buyable upgrade. Angle: Brilliant proved builders will spec smart controls when it helps homes sell — iotty at 2–3 switches per home is a cheap wow in the model home. They care about: per-unit pricing at volume, availability dates, and warranty/service so they don't own callbacks. Longer cycle: aim for 2–3 builder conversations in the pilot, not closed volume.

**Interior designers / architects** — *the spec influencers.* Pain: clients ask for smart home but designers hate visible tech. Angle: this is the one switch that belongs on a mood board — Italian tempered glass, black or white, customizable backlight. Economics: designers mostly won't buy volume; they spec and refer. Give them Trade-tier pricing for client purchases plus a **referral path** (their electrician of record gets fast-tracked as a partner). Find them: ASID/IIDA chapters, Instagram/Pinterest design accounts, local design-build studios.

## 7. The Outreach Engine: Introduce → Entice → Sell

### Introduce (weeks 1–2, then always-on)

The landing page at iottytradepartners.com is the whole first act: hero shot of the glass switch in a designed room, the margin math table, the three tiers, the Founding Partner counter ("37 of 50 slots remaining"), and a 5-minute application. Every outreach touch points here — the page does the explaining so the emails can stay short.

Round 1 runs **phone and email together as co-primary channels** in a 14-day, 8-touch cadence (Email 1 in the morning, Call 1 behind it the same afternoon; four calls and four emails total per contact — full scripts, voicemails, and call windows live in the Round 1 Outreach Playbook). Contacts come from license databases and the CEDIA directory, worked in batches of ~50/week. Supporting channels: **LinkedIn** touches to owners/principals (Day 3 of the cadence), **trade communities and Facebook groups** (soft-touch, value-first posts, not spam), and a short **PR/announcement push** to CE Pro-type trade press about the program launch. Calling matters for this audience specifically: electricians and remodelers pick up in truck-time windows (6:30–8:00 AM, 3:30–5:30 PM local) and a live conversation converts to an application at multiples of cold email alone.

### Entice (the hook inside every touch)

Lead with partner economics and scarcity, never with product specs. The three hooks, matched to what each reader cares about: **margin** ("40 pts on a switch your customer will show their friends"), **differentiation** ("be the only installer in your metro listed on our directory"), and **founding status** ("50 founding slots nationally, pricing locked 12 months"). The free demo-kit switch is the low-commitment yes: "apply, and your first glass switch is on us."

### Sell (application → first order)

Every approved application gets a **15-minute welcome call within 48 hours** — this call is the pilot's highest-leverage activity and should be treated as a sales call, not onboarding admin. Flow: confirm their trade and typical project → show the margin math on *their* typical job → close the demo kit ("install it in your own place this weekend") → book a 3-week follow-up tied to a real upcoming project.

Objection handling to script in advance: *"My guys are Lutron-certified"* (→ additive premium line, not a rip-and-replace; no certification needed — it's a standard box install). *"Wi-Fi switches are unreliable"* (→ direct 2.4GHz Wi-Fi with no hub to fail, UL listed #E527071 + TUV, 1-year limited warranty; concede the honest limits — this builds trust with electricians). *"It's $130 for a switch"* (→ that's the point: you're not selling a switch, you're selling a $45-margin upgrade on labor you're already billing). *"It's pre-order"* (→ that's why founding pricing exists; demo kits ship first / confirm ship dates with Bryan).

## 8. The Hermes Prospecting Engine (Data + Outreach Infrastructure)

The pilot doesn't run on a static list — it runs on the **Hermes agent stack** (already proven on the SmoreLeads scraper build) operating as a daily prospecting engine with a goal of **50 new qualified trade contacts per day** (~250/week, ~1,000/month), feeding the same profile database the future site runs on.

**Sourcing (agents, daily):** state electrical-license databases, CEDIA and HTA directories, ASID/IIDA member rosters, Google Maps/reviews for design-build and integration firms, and permit-activity signals where available. Each segment gets its own collector so daily volume can be steered (e.g., 25 electricians, 15 integrators, 5 builders, 5 designers).

**Profile-building database:** every contact lands as an enriched profile — company, principal name, segment, metro/state, license #, email, phone, website/socials, review volume, project focus — plus a **fit score** that ranks who deserves a phone touch. One Supabase database (the same stack as LeadOS and the SvelteKit site) so prospecting, outreach, and the site share a single source of truth.

**Outreach handling:** Hermes drafts and schedules the email touches of the Round 1 cadence per contact, watches for replies, and sets dispositions; the **daily call queue is auto-built** from the top-scored contacts due for a phone touch. Every dial's outcome (connect, voicemail, objection heard, DNC) logs back to the profile, so the cadence position is always current and no contact gets double-touched.

**Built into the site:** the SvelteKit build at iottytradepartners.com and the prospecting database are one system. The partner application writes into the same table Hermes prospects into (a prospect who applies simply changes status); approval auto-generates the directory listing; the founding-slot counter on the landing page reads live from the database; and an admin view shows the full funnel — prospected → contacted → applied → approved → activated → ordering.

**Capacity reality:** at 50/day, phone time is the constraint, not data. The cadence emails run for everyone; call blocks work only the top ~15–20 scored contacts per day. Pilot funnel targets in §5 stay as floors — with Hermes volume behind them, applications should overshoot 150, and the constraint to watch becomes welcome-call throughput.

## 9. Managing the Channel

**Ordering mechanics.** Simplest pilot-grade setup: run trade orders through the existing Shopify store using tagged B2B customer accounts with automatic tier discounts (Shopify's B2B/company profiles or tier-specific discount codes). No separate wholesale portal until volume justifies it. Elite Net-30 can be handled with draft orders + invoicing. Decision needed from Bryan: whether trade orders ship from the same US fulfillment path as D2C.

**Onboarding sequence (automated, 5 touches over 30 days):** welcome + directory listing confirmation → demo kit install guide + asset kit (logos, photography, spec sheets, a one-page client-facing sell sheet) → margin math + how-to-quote guide → case study/social proof → 30-day check-in booking link.

**Operating cadence.** Monthly partner email (new products, top-partner spotlight, install photos). Quarterly business review calls for Pro/Elite (pipeline, upcoming projects, feedback to product). A simple partner-health score — days since last order, demo kit installed y/n, directory clicks — flags who gets a call each week. During the pilot this is a spreadsheet/CRM view Ryan works weekly; it does not need software.

**Rules of the road (set these before the first partner signs):** MAP policy (partners don't advertise below MSRP online — protects everyone's margin), directory listing standards, demo-kit limits (one per company), and a clear returns/warranty flow so partners never eat a defective unit (dead-on-arrival units replaced ahead of return, Elite gets advance replacement).

## 10. 90-Day Timeline

**Weeks 1–2 — Build:** finalize tiers/pricing with Bryan, landing page + application live on iottytradepartners.com, asset kit and sell sheet done, prospect list built (target: 500 named contacts across the four segments), sequences written.
**Weeks 3–6 — Introduce & Entice:** call + email cadence live (batches of ~50/week, two daily call blocks in trade windows), trade-community seeding, PR note out; goal: 75 applications, 25 approvals, first demo kits shipped.
**Weeks 7–10 — Sell:** welcome calls at full cadence, follow-ups tied to real projects, first project orders land; publish first partner spotlight; goal: fill remaining Founding slots.
**Weeks 11–13 — Manage & Measure:** reorder pushes, QBR-lite calls with top 10, exit interviews with non-activated partners, day-90 readout against the funnel table in §5, scale/iterate decision.
